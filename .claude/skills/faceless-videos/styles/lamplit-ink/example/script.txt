You live on the fortieth floor.

At ten past nine, the city switches off. Not your flat. The whole city. Block by block, like someone walking through a house, turning off the lamps.

Then yours.

Your phone gets one message before the network dies. Grid failure. Expect days, not hours. Stay home. Stay warm.

First problem. Water.

City pressure only pushes water up about six floors. Above that, electric pumps do the work. You are on floor forty. Whatever is left in the pipes is all you get.

So you fill the bath. Every pot. Every bottle. Then the tap coughs, and stops.

A full bath is about forty gallons. One person needs a gallon a day. You just bought yourself a month.

Second problem. Cold.

It is January. By the second morning, it is eight degrees inside, and falling.

So you shrink your world. You pick the smallest room, nail blankets over the door, and live in there.

One candle puts out about as much heat as an old light bulb. It will not warm a room. It will warm your hands.

Do not light the gas stove for heat. Do not bring the barbecue inside. Carbon monoxide has no smell, no colour, and it does its work while you sleep.

From up here, you can see the whole city. None of it is lit.

Until night three. Across the river, one window comes on. Fortieth floor. Same as you.

You hold your candle to the glass, and cover it with your hand. Once. Twice. Three times.

Across the river, the light blinks back. Three times.

Third problem. In a dark city, a single candle can be seen from more than a mile away. Everyone out there knows exactly which window is yours.

Night five. Water, thirty-three gallons. Candles, nine. Phone, four percent, kept in an inside pocket, because cold eats batteries.

The light is not across the river anymore. It is two streets away. Fortieth floor.

Night seven. It is in the building across the street. You can see the room. A lamp. A sofa. Someone standing at the window, holding a candle. In a yellow beanie. Like yours.

Night nine. The building across the street is dark. And under your front door, there is a thin line of warm light.

You blow out your candle.

The line under the door stays on.
