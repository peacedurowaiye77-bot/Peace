# The Louvre Heist: the 13 image prompts

These are the exact prompts the example's stills were made from (Nano Banana Pro, 16:9, 2K). Each one was sent with
the style sheet attached as an image reference. Here that file is `SKILL/styles/clay-atlas/style-sheet.jpg`.
They work in any image tool that takes a reference image, such as Higgsfield, Nano Banana, GPT Image, Midjourney or Flux.
Save each result as `stills/<name>.jpg` (or .png), then make its depth map: `SKILL/scripts/py SKILL/scripts/depth.py stills`.

Recipe: **subject + camera + light**, then the fixed STYLE line at the end. Keep one idea per still, leave empty space where a
label or date will go, and never ask for text, since every word on screen is drawn in code.

## map (data/map.png, cleaned into map_clean / map_base / map_mask)

Top-down flat vintage parchment map of central Paris around the Louvre, in the parchment-map style of the attached style sheet (aged tan parchment paper texture, muted slate grey-blue water, thin brown hairline streets, faint darker tan city blocks). North is up. The river Seine runs horizontally across the lower third of the frame with its stone quays, the bridges Pont du Carrousel, Pont des Arts and Pont Neuf crossing it, and the pointed western tip of the Ile de la Cite at the lower right. The Louvre palace complex sits just above the river in the centre: the square Cour Carree on the right, the long riverside wing running west along the Seine, the northern wing along Rue de Rivoli and the open Cour Napoleon courtyard between them; the Louvre buildings are filled flat warm orange (#E08A3C) with a thin red (#D2382C) outline. The Tuileries garden is a pale area at the far left. Clean flat cartographic top-down view, soft vignette at the edges, no 3D, no perspective. No text, no labels, no letters, no numbers, no compass, no legend, no watermark.

The model drew street and place names on it anyway, and some were wrong. That is the lesson: erase every bit of text from a
generated map (`data/map_raw_with_ai_labels.jpg` is the raw one; `SKILL/scripts/py tools/clean_map.py data/map.png` paints the
dark text pixels out), then draw the labels you need in code (`mapLabel()` in src/gfx.js), so you control every word and where it sits.
